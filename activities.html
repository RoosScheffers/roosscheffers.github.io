Workshops

Cognitive and Empirical Research in Computational Argumentation (CERCA 2026)

Co-located with COMMA 2026, IIIIA-CSIC, Barcelona. The goal of this workshop was to create a space for researchers working with empirical cognitive methods on argumentation to share materials, present ideas, and test studies. Sharing ideas will facilitate further studies, collaborations, and make it easier for interested researchers to get involved. Therefore, the workshop is also open to any researchers interested in the topic and those who have ideas that others might be able to test.
[Proceedings too be published]

Reasoning with Evidence in Law Enforcement and Forensics (RELAF 2026)

Co-located with ICAIL 2026, SMU, Singapore. RELAF2026 explored how artificial intelligence can advance forensic analysis, digital evidence analysis, and evidential reasoning in judicial and law enforcement contexts.
[Proceedings]

AI for Evidential Reasoning (AI4EVIR 2026)

Co-located with JURIX 2025, University of Turin. The AI for Evidential Reasoning workshop brought together researchers working on formal, computational, and empirical approaches to reasoning with evidence, as well as those with expertise in forensic science and evidence evaluation. With the goal to foster discussion and exchange between theoretical and applied perspectives on how AI can contribute to evidential reasoning in legal and investigative contexts.
[2025 Proceedings]

Computational Models of Natural argument (CMNA 2025 and 2026)

CMNA is a yearly workshop series since 2001. I joined the organising committee in 2025. The workshop focuses on the issue of modelling "natural" argumentation. Naturalness may for example, involve the use of means which are more visual than linguistic to illustrate a point, such as graphics or multimedia, the use of more sophisticated rhetorical devices, interacting at various layers of abstraction, or the exploitation of "extra-rational" characteristics of the audience, taking into account emotions and affective factors.

Events

Fighting Crime Panel

I moderated and was one of the organizers of a panel session on 'Fighting Crime with AI' during the Utrecht AI Event. The session was a collaboration between three Utrecht AI Labs: the AI4Oversight Lab, the AI & Finance Lab, and the National Police Lab AI. All three labs research AI applications for crime detection and run during their work and research into similar dilemmas.
